The Seven Tears

The stations that recorded the pulse, and the seven concentrations on Dr Menon’s map.

Stations

  • Indian seismic station One of four stations whose readings Kairavi aligns overnight; its recording of the pulse shows a longer tail.
  • Chilean seismic station One of the four stations Kairavi aligns overnight; its reading of the pulse carries a second low-frequency band.
  • Kenyan seismic station Registered the pulse four seconds before Australia, as though it lay closer to a source that does not exist.
  • Australian seismic station One of the four stations Kairavi aligns overnight; it registered the pulse four seconds after Kenya.
  • Indonesian seismic stations Among the stations confirming the signal: real, unlike any recorded earthquake, and with no common epicentre.

Concentrations

  • Over northern India The Asian concentration on Dr Menon’s map, covering much of northern India, where the newest signals are not passing through but gathering.
  • Over Africa One of the seven vast pressure concentrations on Dr Menon’s map, roughly one per continent.
  • Over Europe One of the seven vast pressure concentrations on Dr Menon’s map, roughly one per continent.
  • Over North America One of the seven vast pressure concentrations on Dr Menon’s map, roughly one per continent.
  • Over South America One of the seven vast pressure concentrations on Dr Menon’s map, roughly one per continent.
  • Over Australia One of the seven vast pressure concentrations on Dr Menon’s map, roughly one per continent.
  • Over Antarctica One of the seven concentrations on Dr Menon’s map; Antarctica’s has the weakest coverage and the worst confidence.

Places

  • Samar Chemicals The industrial and housekeeping chemicals shop Samyukt’s late father founded; Samyukt haggles with a supplier, chases a stuck invoice and faces Mr Sethi here.
  • The market The bright, crowded market around the shop: pharmacies, coaching centres, food stalls and mobile shops, with scooters squeezing through gaps that do not exist.
  • Samyukt’s home The rented two-bedroom flat where Samyukt lives with Naina and Ishita; from its balcony he feels the night air turn heavier.
  • Kairavi’s office The international company’s office where Kairavi finds a filter erasing Romanised Hindi and stops a flawed model being called ‘production-ready’.
  • Stall under the metro A crowded stall beneath the metro line where Kairavi and Mitali toast reduced accuracy, until Rajeev calls asking for fifteen thousand rupees.
  • The metro line The Delhi Metro’s raised tracks on concrete pillars, plastered with old posters and humming with trains arriving overhead.
  • Kairavi’s home The fourth-floor flat where Kairavi lives with Rajeev, Meera and Aarohi; over dinner she asks that Aarohi be allowed to choose her own course.
  • Rajeev’s supermarket Rajeev’s supermarket, where Kairavi once put back a pair of headphones that he later brought home for her.
  • The flat Raghav lost The ninth-floor flat with good sunlight and terrible tiles that Raghav loses to a family with faster banking reflexes.
  • Raghav’s meeting The conference room where Raghav shows six senior managers why their software migration plan will fail, and how to save it.
  • Kabir’s lab Kabir’s office electronics lab, where his low-cost vibration module picks up the seismic pattern on an isolated test rig, three runs out of three.
  • The roll stall The stall across the market that sends the rolls Raghav and Kabir eat with Samyukt at his desk, before the talk about money.
  • The parking area Where Kabir’s battery-less module glows, and Raghav hears his own name in Kabir’s voice though Kabir’s mouth never moved.
  • The clinic Where, after twelve days of fever, every test comes back normal and a doctor orders Samyukt to rest for two days.
  • The archery range The range where Samyukt trained for years; rusty after three years away, he buries one arrow in the target up to its fletching.
